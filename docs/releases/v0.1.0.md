## BackRight 0.1.0 — first public release

A small Mac app that watches your posture through the webcam and gives you a faint nudge when you slip. **The video never leaves your Mac:** it's analysed on-device, never saved, and the app works with Wi-Fi off — the first-run walkthrough lets you try that yourself.

### What's in it
- **Calibrate once, then forget it.** BackRight learns what *your* good posture looks like and nudges you with a soft glow around the screen edges (plus a small pill with *Pause* and *Settings*) when you drift.
- **Sitting and standing.** Separate calibration for each, for sit-stand desks.
- **Illustrated desk-setup guide** — screen height, chair, arms, and a camera check.
- **Status at a glance** in the menu bar and the Dock (green = monitoring, amber = nudging, grey = snoozed/off), and a Snooze option.
- **Private by design.** No account, no server, no analytics. The app blocks every outside network connection; the first-run walkthrough shows a live counter you can watch while you turn Wi-Fi off.

### Install
Download **BackRight-0.1.0-arm64.dmg** below, open it and drag **BackRight** to **Applications**.

BackRight is free and open source and is **not notarized by Apple** (that needs a paid developer account), so macOS blocks the first launch:
1. Open BackRight. When macOS says it can't verify the app, click **Done**.
2. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll to **Security**, and click **Open Anyway** next to the BackRight message. Enter your password.  
   (macOS 14 or earlier: right-click the app → **Open** → **Open**.)
3. Allow camera access when asked.

Prefer not to trust a download? Read the code and build it yourself — see the [README](https://github.com/lylerbaumgarten/backright#run-it).

### Requirements
- macOS 13 or newer on a Mac with **Apple Silicon** (M1 or later). Intel Macs: build from source for now.
- A webcam that can see your head and shoulders.

### Known limitations
- Early release — expect rough edges. Only tested on Apple Silicon Macs running recent macOS.
- Uses a front-facing webcam, so it measures head position, leaning and shoulder level against your calibration. It can't see your lower body, so knee/hip checks in the desk guide are best-effort.
- One person in frame; works best with decent lighting.
- Windows isn't supported yet.
- **Not a medical device.** BackRight is an ergonomics reminder, not medical advice. If you have pain, see a professional.
